Write a 450–550-word essay for a U.S. high-school history class.

Question: Why was the civil rights movement important in the United States?

Your finished answer must have a title, exactly five paragraphs, and a short Sources list. Follow the steps below in order.

STEP 1: READ THE WRITING GUIDANCE

Find the installed education agent skills. Read the instructions relevant to researching a topic and writing an essay. Apply those instructions along with the requirements below.

STEP 2: RESEARCH WITH OFFLINE WIKIPEDIA

Use the OpenZIM/Wikipedia tool to find and read these articles:
- Civil rights movement
- Rosa Parks
- Montgomery bus boycott

Before writing, identify information from the articles about:
- Racial segregation and its effects on daily life.
- The movement’s goal of equal rights.
- Rosa Parks’s arrest and the community’s response.
- What the Montgomery bus boycott sought to change and what happened afterward.
- One specific legal change, such as the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

Use the retrieved articles to check the dates, names, and descriptions you include. Do not rely on your memory when a source can be checked. Do not invent quotations or citations.

STEP 3: WRITE THE ESSAY

Give the essay a short, informative title. Then write five paragraphs in the following order. Aim for approximately 80–110 words per paragraph; the whole essay should be 450–550 words, not counting the title or Sources list.

Paragraph 1 — Introduction:
- Begin with one or two sentences explaining that many Black Americans faced unequal treatment and segregation in the mid-20th-century United States.
- Introduce the civil rights movement as an organized effort to challenge that inequality.
- End the paragraph with a clear main argument: the movement was important because ordinary people challenged segregation and helped bring about lasting legal change.
- Do not begin with “Throughout history” or a broad statement unrelated to the question.

Paragraph 2 — The problem:
- Begin with a sentence defining segregation in simple terms.
- Explain how segregation affected everyday life, using one or two specific examples supported by the articles.
- Explain why unequal access and treatment gave people a reason to organize.
- End by connecting this problem to the next paragraph about the bus boycott.

Paragraph 3 — The example:
- Begin by introducing Rosa Parks and the Montgomery bus boycott as an example of people challenging segregation.
- Explain Parks’s action and the community response in the correct order.
- Explain how the boycott put pressure on the segregated bus system. Do not imply Parks acted alone or that her action alone ended segregation.
- End with a sentence explaining how this example supports the essay’s main argument.

Paragraph 4 — The change:
- Begin by explaining that the movement also sought changes beyond Montgomery.
- Describe one specific legal change supported by the articles, such as the Civil Rights Act of 1964. Briefly explain what it addressed.
- Connect that change to the movement’s broader goal of equal rights. Do not claim that one law immediately ended all discrimination.
- End by explaining why this change was significant.

Paragraph 5 — Conclusion:
- Restate the main argument in different words; do not copy the introduction.
- Briefly connect the boycott and the legal change as examples of collective action.
- Finish with a clear sentence about why understanding the movement matters today. Do not introduce a new historical event or unsupported claim.

STEP 4: CHECK YOUR WORK

Read the whole essay once. Check that each paragraph follows from the one before it, that sentences are complete, and that the explanation connects evidence to the main argument. Use straightforward words and varied sentence lengths. Avoid bullet points in the essay, repetition, exaggerated claims, and unnecessarily formal vocabulary.
